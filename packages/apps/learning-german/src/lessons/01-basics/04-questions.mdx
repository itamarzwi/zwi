## Types of questions

German has two types of questions:
1. Yes/no questions
2. Question-word questions (or "W-questions").

Yes/no questions are simple questions that can be answered with a single "yes" or "no":


<ExampleList>
  <Example en="Are you learning German?" />
  <Example en="Does Anna live in Marburg?" />
  <Example en="Is the book new?" />
</ExampleList>


"W-questions", like who, where or when, ask questions that always require full sentence answers:

<ExampleList>
  <Example en="Where do you live?" />
  <Example en="Who is Anna?" />
  <Example en="Why are you learning German?" />
</ExampleList>

In German, W-questions have their own sentence structure, while yes/no questions follow a modified version of the basic sentence structure

## Yes/no questions

To turn a statement into a yes/no question, all you have to do is move the verb over to the first position:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Du verb(lernst) Deutsch." en="You are verb(learning) German." />
  <Example de="verb(Lernst) du Deutsch?" en="Are you verb(learning) German?" />
</ExampleList>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Anna verb(wohnt) in Marburg." en="Anna verb(lives) in Marburg" />
  <Example de="verb(Wohnt) Anna in Marburg?" en="verb(Does) Anna verb(live) in Marburg?" />
</ExampleList>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Das Buch verb(ist) neu." en="The book verb(is) new" />
  <Example de="verb(Ist) das Buch neu?" en="verb(Is) the book new?" />
</ExampleList>

While the German version always looks the same, English has many different forms for yes/no questions. The above examples demonstrate this.


German has no word like "do" or "does", the verb simply jumps to the first position.

## W-question words

A W-question starts with a W-question word. Here are some common ones:

| German | English |
| ------ | ------- |
| wer? | who? |
| was? | what? |
| wo? | where? |
| woher? | where from? |
| wohin? | where to? |
| wann? | when? |
| wie? | how? |
| warum? | why? |
| wie viel? | how much? |
| wie viele? | how many? |

"woher" and "wohin" do not translate 1-to-1 with how you would use them in English. English separates these words and puts the second part at the end: "where ... from" and "where ... to". German keeps the word whole.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="adverb(Woher) kommst du?" en="adverb(Where) are you adverb(from)?" />
  <Example de="adverb(Wohin) gehst du?" en="adverb(Where) are you going adverb(to)?" />
</ExampleList>

<Callout>

</Callout>

Zum Beispiel¹, in English you would say "Where are you from?", not "Where from are you?", but in German you say "Woher kommst du?"

<Footnote>1. Zum Beispiel means "for example".</Footnote>

## W-questions

In a W-question the question word comes first and the verb stays in second position, just like in a statement. The subject follows the verb.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wo verb(wohnst) du?" en="Where do you live?" />
  <Example de="Woher verb(kommt) Anna?" en="Where is Anna from?" />
  <Example de="Wann verb(lernst) du Deutsch?" en="When do you learn German?" />
  <Example de="Wie verb(heißt) du?" en="What is your name?" />
  <Example de="Warum verb(lernst) du Deutsch?" en="Why are you learning German?" />
</ExampleList>

Wie heißt du? literally means "How are you called?", which is why German uses wie there and not was.

<Callout title="Yes/no or W-question?">
Look at the first word. If it is a verb, the question is yes/no. If it is a question word, the verb comes second.
</Callout>

### Wo, woher, wohin

These three all ask about a place, but they ask different things. Wo asks where something is, woher asks where it comes from, and wohin asks where it is going.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wo wohnst du?" en="Where do you live?" note="No movement, just a place." />
  <Example de="Woher kommst du?" en="Where are you from?" note="The starting point." />
  <Example de="Wohin gehst du?" en="Where are you going?" note="The destination." />
</ExampleList>

### Wie viel and wie viele

Wie viel is for things you can't count one by one, like money or time. It never changes. Wie viele is for countable things and goes with a plural noun.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wie viel Zeit hast du?" en="How much time do you have?" />
  <Example de="Wie viele Bücher hast du?" en="How many books do you have?" />
</ExampleList>

## Wer and was

Wer and was ask about people and things. Like any other noun, what they stand for has a case. If you ask about the subject, you use wer or was. If you ask about the direct object, the Akkusativ, was stays the same, but wer becomes wen.

<GridTable>

| Case | person | thing |
| ---- | ------ | ----- |
| Nom. | wer | was |
| Akk. | wen | was |

</GridTable>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Wer) sieht den Hund?" en="green(Who) sees the dog?" />
  <Example de="blue(Wen) sieht der Mann?" en="blue(Whom) does the man see?" />
  <Example de="green(Was) liegt auf dem Tisch?" en="green(What) is lying on the table?" />
  <Example de="blue(Was) liest das Kind?" en="blue(What) is the child reading?" />
</ExampleList>

Asking "who or what?" finds the subject, and "whom or what?" finds the object. These are the same two questions you used to find the cases.
