## Du or Sie?

German has two words for saying "you" to one person. Du is informal, Sie is formal.

Use du with family, friends, and people in casual settings. Use Sie with strangers, formal interactions, and with anyone you would address as "Mr." or "Ms.".

Sie is always written with a capital S, and it takes the same verb form as "they" (sie):

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wie heißt green(du)?" en="What is your name? (informal)" />
  <Example de="Wie heißen green(Sie)?" en="What is your name? (formal)" />
</ExampleList>

When talking to a group of people, the casual form of "you (plural)" is ihr, and the formal form of "you (plural)" is Sie. The formal "you" is the same whether you talk to one person or to many.

## Pronouns and cases

Pronouns replace nouns, so they have a case too. The ones you know so far are the Nominativ forms. In the Akkusativ, most of them change:

| English      | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
| ------------ | --------- | --------- |
| I / me       | ich       | mich      |
| you          | du        | dich      |
| he / him     | er        | ihn       |
| she / her    | sie       | sie       |
| it           | es        | es        |
| we / us      | wir       | uns       |
| you (pl.)    | ihr       | euch      |
| they / them  | sie       | sie       |
| you (formal) | Sie       | Sie       |

<Callout title="Er, sie, es are not only for people">
  German pronouns follow the gender of the noun they replace. A masculine thing is "er", a feminine thing is "sie", a neuter thing is "es": Der Hund is "er", die Katze is "sie", das Buch is "es". In English all of them are just "it".
</Callout>

## Using the Akkusativ pronouns

The object pronoun takes the same spot as the noun it replaces, right after the verb.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich sehe blue(den Hund)." en="I see the dog." />
  <Example de="Ich sehe blue(ihn)." en="I see him." />
  <Example de="Anna kennt blue(die Frau)." en="Anna knows the woman." />
  <Example de="Anna kennt blue(sie)." en="Anna knows her." />
  <Example de="Liebst green(du) blue(mich)?" en="Do you love me?" />
  <Example de="Ich brauche blue(dich)." en="I need you." />
  <Example de="Wir besuchen blue(euch) morgen." en="We are visiting you tomorrow." />
</ExampleList>

Since pronouns follow the gender of the noun, the same forms work for animals and things:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe einen Hund. Ich liebe blue(ihn)." en="I have a dog. I love him." />
  <Example de="Das Buch ist gut. Ich lese blue(es)." en="The book is good. I am reading it." />
</ExampleList>

## Two words in the Dativ

You will hear the Dativ forms of ich and du very early, in a few fixed phrases. You already saw dir in "Wie geht es dir?". We will learn the Dativ case later, but for now you should learn these: mir (to me) and dir (to you).

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wie geht es orange(dir)?" en="How are you?" />
  <Example de="orange(Mir) ist kalt." en="I am cold." />
  <Example de="Das gefällt orange(mir)." en="I like that." />
</ExampleList>

The formal "How are you?" is "Wie geht es Ihnen?", with the Dativ form of Sie - Ihnen.
