## Pronouns and cases

Pronouns replace nouns, so they has a case too. You already know the default, Nominativ ones. In the Akkusativ, most of them change:

| English | Nominativ | Akkusativ |
| ------- | --------- | --------- |
| I / me | ich | mich |
| you | du | dich |
| he / him | er | ihn |
| she / her | sie | sie |
| it | es | es |
| we / us | wir | uns |
| you (pl.) | ihr | euch |
| they / them | sie | sie |
| you (formal) | Sie | Sie |

<Callout title="Er, sie, es are not only for people">
  German pronouns follow the gender of the noun they replace. A masculine thing is "er", a feminine thing is "sie", a neuter thing is "es": Der Hund is "er", die Katze is "sie", das Buch is "es". In English all of them are just "it".
</Callout>

## Using the Akkusativ pronouns

The object pronoun takes the same spot as the noun it replaces, right after the verb.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich sehe blue(den Hund)." en="I see the dog." />
  <Example de="Ich sehe blue(ihn)." en="I see him." />
  <Example de="Anna kennt blue(die Frau)." en="Anna knows the woman." />
  <Example de="Anna kennt blue(sie)." en="Anna knows her." />
  <Example de="Liebst green(du) blue(mich)?" en="Do you love me?" />
  <Example de="Ich brauche blue(dich)." en="I need you." />
  <Example de="Wir besuchen blue(euch) morgen." en="We are visiting you tomorrow." />
</ExampleList>

Because "ihn" and "es" can mean things as well as people, you can say it about objects too:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe einen Hund. Ich liebe blue(ihn)." en="I have a dog. I love him." />
  <Example de="Das Buch ist gut. Ich lese blue(es)." en="The book is good. I am reading it." />
</ExampleList>

## Two words in the Dativ

You will hear the Dativ forms of ich and du very early, in a few fixed phrases. Dativ is a case we haven't covered yet, so for now just learn these as chunks: mir (to me) and dir (to you).

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wie geht es blue(dir)?" en="How are you? (informal)" />
  <Example de="Gut, danke. Und blue(dir)?" en="Good, thanks. And you?" />
  <Example de="Mir ist kalt." en="I am cold." />
  <Example de="Das gefällt blue(mir)." en="I like that." />
</ExampleList>

The formal "How are you?" is Wie geht es Ihnen?, with another Dativ form, Ihnen.
