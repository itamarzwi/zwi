## Nouns have genders

Every German noun is masculine, feminine or neuter. The gender shows up in the word for "the":

<GenderedTable
  label=""
  rows={[
    ["Article", "der", "das", "die"],
    ["Example", "der Mann (the man)", "das Kind (the child)", "die Frau (the woman)"],
  ]}
/>

<Callout title="Learn the article with the noun">
The gender is usually not guessable. Always learn a noun together with its article: not "Stadt", but **die Stadt**.
</Callout>

## Nouns are capitalised

Unlike English, **all nouns** start with a capital letter, wherever they are in the sentence.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Die **Frau** hat einen **Hund**." en="The **woman** has a **dog**." />
  <Example de="Der **Mann** liest ein **Buch**." en="The **man** reads a **book**." />
  <Example de="Das **Kind** trinkt **Wasser**." en="The **child** drinks **water**." />
</ExampleList>

## Verb forms

Verbs change their ending depending on who does the action. Most verbs follow the same pattern, for example heißen (to be called) and kommen (to come):

| Person | English | heißen | kommen |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | ------ |
| ich | I | heiße | komme |
| du | you | heißt | kommst |
| er | he | heißt | kommt |
| sie | she | heißt | kommt |
| es | it | heißt | kommt |
| wir | we | heißen | kommen |
| ihr | you all | heißt | kommt |
| sie | they | heißen | kommen |
| Sie | you (formal) | heißen | kommen |

<Callout title="er/sie/es">
Look at the table: er, sie and es always take the exact same verb form. From now on, tables will show them as one row: er/sie/es.
</Callout>

### Irregular verbs

A few very common verbs are irregular and have to be learned by heart, like sein (to be) and können (can, to be able to):

| Person | English | sein | können |
| ------ | ------- | ---- | ------ |
| ich | I | bin | kann |
| du | you | bist | kannst |
| er/sie/es | he/she/it | ist | kann |
| wir | we | sind | können |
| ihr | you all | seid | könnt |
| sie | they | sind | können |
| Sie | you (formal) | sind | können |

## Numbers 1-10

Let's start counting. Numbers are short and worth learning by heart:

| Number | German |
| ------ | ------ |
| 1 | eins |
| 2 | zwei |
| 3 | drei |
| 4 | vier |
| 5 | fünf |
| 6 | sechs |
| 7 | sieben |
| 8 | acht |
| 9 | neun |
| 10 | zehn |

<Callout title="eins or ein?">
Counting on its own, you say **eins**. Before a noun, it becomes **ein** (or **eine**, as with articles): **ein** Hund, **eine** Frau.
</Callout>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe **zwei** Hunde." en="I have **two** dogs." />
  <Example de="Das Kind ist **acht** Jahre alt." en="The child is **eight** years old." />
</ExampleList>

## Possessive adjectives

"My", "your" and so on are placed in front of the noun. Each one belongs to a person:

| Person | English | German | Example |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | ------- |
| ich | my | mein | mein Name |
| du | your | dein | dein Name |
| er | his | sein | sein Name |
| sie | her | ihr | ihr Name |
| es | its | sein | sein Name |
| wir | our | unser | unser Name |
| ihr | your (you all) | euer | euer Name |
| sie | their | ihr | ihr Name |
| Sie | your (formal) | Ihr | Ihr Name |

<Callout title="Same word with a different meaning">
ihr can mean "her" or "their", and Ihr (capital I) is the formal "your". The capital letter and the context tell them apart.
</Callout>

### Endings

With feminine nouns (and plurals) an **-e** is added: mein**e** Stadt, dein**e** Frau. Masculine and neuter nouns get no ending. With euer, the middle "e" drops when an ending is added: **eure** Stadt.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Das ist **mein** Hund." en="That is **my** dog." />
  <Example de="Das ist **meine** Frau." en="That is **my** wife." />
  <Example de="**Unser** Kind heißt Anna." en="**Our** child is called Anna." />
  <Example de="**Euer** Hund ist klein." en="**Your** dog is small." />
  <Example de="**Eure** Stadt ist groß." en="**Your** city is big." />
</ExampleList>
