## Dativ pronouns

You already know the pronouns in the Nominativ and the Akkusativ case (see: [Pronouns and cases](/lessons/01-basics/06-pronouns?page=2)). And you've met the dative forms of ich and du in "Wie geht es dir?" and "Mir ist kalt". Here is the expanded pronouns table with the Dativ form:

| English      | Nominativ | Akkusativ | Dativ |
| ------------ | --------- | --------- | ----- |
| I / me       | ich       | mich      | mir   |
| you          | du        | dich      | dir   |
| he / him     | er        | ihn       | ihm   |
| she / her    | sie       | sie       | ihr   |
| it           | es        | es        | ihm   |
| we / us      | wir       | uns       | uns   |
| you (pl.)    | ihr       | euch      | euch  |
| they / them  | sie       | sie       | ihnen |
| you (formal) | Sie       | Sie       | Ihnen |

Here's 2 observations that can make this easier to remember:

1. -r and -m endings are indicative of the Dativ case. This covers all of the first 5 rows! 
2. we / you (pl.) have the same pronouns in the Akkusativ and the Dativ.

Examples:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) gebe orange(dem Mann) blue(den Schlüssel)." en="I give the man the key / I give the key to the man." />
  <Example de="green(Ich) gebe orange(ihm) blue(den Schlüssel)." en="I give him the key." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) zeigt orange(der Frau) blue(die Stadt)." en="Anna shows the woman the city." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) zeigt orange(ihr) blue(die Stadt)." en="Anna shows her the city." />
</ExampleList>

<Callout title="Ihr has two jobs">
  Ihr is the Nominativ "you (plural)", and it is also the Dativ of sie (she). "Ihr gebt ihr das Buch" means "You (plural) are giving her the book". The verb helps distinguish here: ihr as the subject comes with the -t verb form (ihr gebt), and the Dativ ihr is the one receiving something.
</Callout>

## Two objects: word order

In the Dativ lesson you saw that with two nouns, the Dativ normally comes first. Pronouns change that. There are three cases, depending on what the two objects are.

### Two nouns: Dativ first

This is the rule you already know:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) gebe orange(dem Mann) blue(den Schlüssel)." en="I give the man the key / I give the key to the man." />
</ExampleList>

### A pronoun and a noun: pronoun first

When one object is a pronoun and the other is a noun, the pronoun comes first, whatever its case:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) gebe orange(ihm) blue(den Schlüssel)." en="I give him the key." note="Dativ pronoun first" />
  <Example de="Ich habe einen Schlüssel. green(Ich) gebe blue(ihn) orange(dem Mann)." en="I have a key. I give it to the man." note="Akkusativ pronoun first." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) zeigt orange(mir) blue(das Foto)." en="Anna shows me the photo." note="Dativ pronoun first" />
  <Example de="Anna hat ein Foto. green(Sie) zeigt blue(es) orange(ihrer Mutter)." en="Anna has a photo. She shows it to her mother." note="Akkusativ pronoun first." />
</ExampleList>

### Thema-Rhema

In German, we put information that is already known before information that is new. The known part is called the Thema (the topic), and the new part is the Rhema (what we are saying about it).  The new information goes towards the end of the sentence, where the emphasis naturally falls. This is called the the **Thema-Rhema-Gliederung** or just **Thema-Rhema**.

A pronoun is always known information. You can only say "es" if everyone already knows what "es" is. That is why pronouns move to the front, before any noun:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Was macht Jess? green(Ich) muss orange(ihm) blue(den Schlüssel) geben." en="What is Jess doing? I need to give him the key." note="We know Jess. The key is the new information." />
</ExampleList>

### Two pronouns: Akkusativ first

When both objects are pronouns, the order flips: the Akkusativ comes first.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) gebe blue(es) orange(dir)." en="I give it to you." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) zeigt blue(es) orange(mir)." en="Anna shows it to me." />
</ExampleList>

Thema and Rhema don't help here, since both pronouns are known information. This one is just a rule to learn. "Ich gebe es dir" is one of the most common sentences you will hear, so if you remember that one, you remember the rule.

### Summary

| Objects             | Order            | Example                          |
| ------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Noun and noun       | Dativ, Akkusativ | Ich gebe dem Mann den Schlüssel. |
| Pronoun and noun    | Pronoun, noun    | Ich gebe ihm den Schlüssel.      |
| Noun and pronoun    | Pronoun, noun    | Ich gebe ihn dem Mann.           |
| Pronoun and pronoun | Akkusativ, Dativ | Ich gebe ihn ihm.                |

<Callout title="The subject is not part of this">
  These rules are only about the two objects. The subject and the verb do their usual thing: the verb is in position 2, and the subject comes right before or right after it. Tomorrow I'll give it to you - "Morgen gebe ich es dir".
</Callout>
