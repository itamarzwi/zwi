## Verbs with a Dativ object

Most verbs with one object take the Akkusativ: ich sehe dich, ich liebe dich, ich kenne dich, etc. But a small group of verbs takes the Dativ instead, even when there is only one object:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) sehe blue(dich)." en="I see you." note="Akkusativ" />
  <Example de="green(Ich) helfe orange(dir)." en="I help you." note="Dativ" />
</ExampleList>

English gives you no hint here, since "help you" and "see you" look exactly the same. You have to learn these verbs as Dativ verbs. The good news is that there are not many of them, and you will mostly use the same few all the time.

## Helfen, danken, antworten, gratulieren

The first group of Dativ verbs we'll look at are verbs about doing something for a person: helping them, thanking them, answering them, congratulating them.

| Verb        | English         | Example              | Translation          |
| ----------- | --------------- | -------------------- | -------------------- |
| helfen      | to help         | Ich helfe dir.       | I help you.          |
| danken      | to thank        | Ich danke Ihnen.     | I thank you.         |
| antworten   | to answer       | Er antwortet mir.    | He answers me.       |
| gratulieren | to congratulate | Wir gratulieren dir! | We congratulate you! |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Jess) hilft orange(seiner Mutter)." en="Jess helps his mother." />
  <Example de="Kannst green(du) orange(uns) helfen?" en="Can you help us?" />
  <Example de="green(Ich) danke orange(dir) für das Geschenk." en="Thank you for the present." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) antwortet orange(dem Lehrer)." en="Anna answers the teacher." />
  <Example de="green(Anna) gratuliert orange(ihrem Bruder)." en="Anna congratulates her brother." />
</ExampleList>

<Callout title="Ich helfe dich">
  This is one of the most common mistakes, because in English "help" works like any other verb. In German, helfen always takes the Dativ: ich helfe **dir**, ich helfe **ihm**, ich helfe **der Frau**.
</Callout>

## Gehören, gefallen, schmecken, passen

The second group of Dativ verbs are verbs where the subject is a thing, and the person is in the Dativ:

| Verb      | English            | Example                 | Translation                 |
| --------- | ------------------ | ----------------------- | --------------------------- |
| gehören   | to belong to       | Das Buch gehört mir.    | The book belongs to me.     |
| gefallen  | to be liked by     | Die Stadt gefällt mir.  | I like the city.            |
| passen    | to fit, to suit    | Die Jacke passt mir.    | The jacket fits me.         |

<Callout title="Gefällt mir">
  We translate "Die Stadt gefällt mir" as "I like the city", instead of "The city is liked by me", because that is the most natural way of saying it in both languages. In English, I is the subject and the city is the object. In German, the city is the subject and I am the object.
</Callout>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Das Buch) gehört orange(meinem Bruder)." en="The book belongs to my brother." />
  <Example de="green(Marburg) gefällt orange(uns) sehr." en="We really like Marburg." />
  <Example de="green(Die Schuhe) passen orange(mir) nicht." en="The shoes don't fit me." />
</ExampleList>

Note that the verb form follows the subject, which in this case is an object:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Der Schuh) gefällt orange(mir)." en="I like the shoe." />
  <Example de="green(Die Schuhe) gefallen orange(mir)." en="I like the shoes." />
</ExampleList>

### Wem gehört das?

When asking "who" in the Dativ, we use wem (to whom). You now know all three forms: wer (Nominativ), wen (Akkusativ) and wem (Dativ).

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="orange(Wem) gehört das Auto?" en="Who does the car belong to?" />
  <Example de="Das Auto gehört orange(meinem Bruder)." en="The car belongs to my brother." />
  <Example de="orange(Wem) hilfst du?" en="Who are you helping?" />
  <Example de="Ich hilfe orange(ihm)." en="I am helping him." />
</ExampleList>

## Gefallen or mögen?

German has two common ways to say "I like". Gefallen is one of them. The other is mögen, which works like a normal verb with an Akkusativ object:

| Person    | mögen  |
| --------- | ------ |
| ich       | mag    |
| du        | magst  |
| er/sie/es | mag    |
| wir       | mögen  |
| ihr       | mögt   |
| sie/Sie   | mögen  |

With mögen the person who likes is the subject. With gefallen the thing that is liked is the subject. The same idea, with the sentence flipped:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) mag blue(den Film)." en="I like the film." note="Ich is the subject" />
  <Example de="green(Der Film) gefällt orange(mir)." en="I like the film." note="Der Film is the subject" />
  <Example de="green(Anna) mag blue(die Stadt)." en="Anna likes the city." />
  <Example de="green(Die Stadt) gefällt orange(Anna)." en="Anna likes the city." />
</ExampleList>

The two are not always interchangeable, and sometimes one is a better fit than the other. But you don't need to worry about that for now. Just now that both versions exist:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="green(Ich) mag blue(Pizza)." en="I like pizza." />
  <Example de="green(Ich) mag blue(dich)." en="I like you." />
  <Example de="green(Die Jacke) gefällt orange(mir)." en="I like the jacket."/>
</ExampleList>
