## Time prepositions

There are three prepositions in German for describing **when** something happes: um, am and im.

| Preposition | Used for               | Example             |
| ----------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| um          | clock times            | um acht Uhr         |
| am          | days, parts of the day | am Montag, am Abend |
| im          | months, seasons        | im Juli, im Sommer  |

The sentence structure rules from before work the same, with the preposition acting as part of the time/place part:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wir essen timeplace(um acht Uhr)." en="We eat timeplace(at eight o'clock)." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Am Montag) arbeite ich nicht." en="timeplace(On Monday) I'm not working." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Im Juli) fliegen wir nach Litauen." en="timeplace(In July) we're flying to Lithuania." />
</ExampleList>

## Clock times: um

To ask for the time, you say "Wie spät ist es?" Literally it translates to "How late is it?" but it is used in the same way as "What time is it?" in English. Another way to ask for the time is "Wie viel Uhr ist es?" (Literally: "How much o'clock is it?"). Of course, you don't have to memorize these now.

In the answer, Uhr goes between the hours and the minutes:

| Time  | German                      |
| ----- | --------------------------- |
| 8:00  | acht Uhr                    |
| 8:15  | acht Uhr fünfzehn           |
| 8:30  | acht Uhr dreißig            |
| 14:45 | vierzehn Uhr fünfundvierzig |

To say that something happens at a certain time, use um. To ask about it, use "Um wie viel Uhr?" (At what time?) or just "Wann?":

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich gehe timeplace(um sieben Uhr) nach Hause." en="I go home timeplace(at seven o'clock)." />
  <Example de="Um wie viel Uhr ist der Film?" en="At what time is the movie?" />
  <Example de="Wann ist der Film?" en="When is the movie?" />
  <Example de="Der Zug fährt timeplace(um 14 Uhr (zwei Uhr))." en="The train leaves timeplace(at 2pm)."  />
</ExampleList>

<Callout title="Written and spoken times">
Written times may use the 24-hour clock: 14 Uhr, 19:30.<br/>
However when speaking, people usually use the 12-hour clock. "14 Uhr" is said as "zwei Uhr", and if it isn't clear from context, you can add the time specification, e.g. nachmittags (in the afternoon): "um zwei Uhr nachmittags".
</Callout>

People also tell the time in a more casual way, like "halb acht" (7:30). That is a more umgangssprachlich¹ way of talking about time. But the way on this page is always correct.  
Careful! Halb acht means "half (to) eight", so 7:30, not 8:30!

<Footnote>1. colloquial.</Footnote>

## Days and parts of the day: am

Use "am" with the days of the week:

| German     | English   |
| ---------- | --------- |
| Montag     | Monday    |
| Dienstag   | Tuesday   |
| Mittwoch   | Wednesday |
| Donnerstag | Thursday  |
| Freitag    | Friday    |
| Samstag    | Saturday  |
| Sonntag    | Sunday    |

All the days are masculine: der Montag, der Dienstag, and so on.

"am" also goes with the parts of the day:

| German        | English             |
| ------------- | ------------------- |
| am Morgen     | in the morning      |
| am Vormittag  | in the late morning |
| am Mittag     | at noon             |
| am Nachmittag | in the afternoon    |
| am Abend      | in the evening      |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="timeplace(Am Samstag) besuche ich meine Freunde." en="timeplace(On Saturday) I'm visiting my friends." />
  <Example de="Jess arbeitet timeplace(am Nachmittag) nicht." en="Jess doesn't work timeplace(in the afternoon)." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Am Sonntag) bin ich zu Hause geblieben." en="timeplace(On Sunday) I stayed at home." />
</ExampleList>

With "heute", "morgen" and "gestern", you don't need a preposition at all: heute Abend (this evening), gestern Abend (last night), morgen Nachmittag (tomorrow afternoon).

<Callout title="Morgen früh">
"Tomorrow morning" is an exception: it is morgen früh, **not** "morgen Morgen". Tomorrow (morgen) and the morning (der Morgen) use the same word, so for "tomorrow morning" we say "morgen früh"!
</Callout>

## Months and seasons: im

Use "im" with months and seasons:

| German    | English   |
| --------- | --------- |
| Januar    | January   |
| Februar   | February  |
| März      | March     |
| April     | April     |
| Mai       | May       |
| Juni      | June      |
| Juli      | July      |
| August    | August    |
| September | September |
| Oktober   | October   |
| November  | November  |
| Dezember  | December  |

| German   | English |
| -------- | ------- |
| Frühling | spring  |
| Sommer   | summer  |
| Herbst   | autumn  |
| Winter   | winter  |

Like the days, all months and seasons are masculine.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="timeplace(Im Dezember) ist es kalt." en="timeplace(In December) it is cold." />
  <Example de="Anna hat timeplace(im Mai) Geburtstag." en="Anna's birthday is timeplace(in May)." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Im Sommer) sind wir nach Litauen geflogen." en="timeplace(In the summer) we flew to Lithuania." />
</ExampleList>

## bis und ab

"bis" means "until", and "ab" means "from" or "starting at", and they can come with any time word.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich arbeite timeplace(bis fünf Uhr)." en="I work timeplace(until five o'clock)." />
</ExampleList>

To give a start and an end, use von ... bis (from ... to):

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich arbeite timeplace(von Montag bis Freitag)." en="I work timeplace(from Monday to Friday)." />
  <Example de="Der Sommer ist timeplace(von Juni bis August)." en="Summer is timeplace(from June to August)." />
  <Example de="Der Kurs ist timeplace(von neun bis zwölf Uhr)." en="The course is timeplace(from nine to twelve)." />
</ExampleList>

You will also hear bis all the time when people say goodbye:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Bis morgen!" en="See you tomorrow!" />
  <Example de="Bis bald!" en="See you soon!" />
  <Example de="Bis später!" en="See you later!" />
</ExampleList>
