## Akkusativ prepositions

Prepositions are small words that, as the name suggests, appear before positions (😱). But gramatically, they always appear before a **noun**. In German, every preposition decides which case the noun after it takes.

Five prepositions always take the Akkusativ, no matter what:

| Preposition | English | Example           |
| ----------- | ------- | ----------------- |
| für         | for     | für meinen Bruder |
| ohne        | without | ohne einen Plan   |
| durch       | through | durch den Park    |
| um          | around  | um den Tisch      |
| gegen       | against | gegen die Wand    |

You already know the Akkusativ forms, so there is nothing new to learn here. Remember that only masculine nouns change: der becomes den, ein becomes einen, mein becomes meinen. Feminine, neuter and plural nouns look the same as in the Nominativ.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Das Geschenk ist für blue(meine Mutter)." en="The present is for my mother." />
  <Example de="Das Geschenk ist für blue(meinen Bruder)." en="The present is for my brother." />
  <Example de="Ich gehe durch blue(den Park)." en="I walk through the park." />
</ExampleList>

## With pronouns

When the preposition is followed by a pronoun, use the Akkusativ pronoun: mich, dich, ihn, sie, es, uns, euch, sie, Sie.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ist das für blue(mich)?" en="Is that for me?" />
  <Example de="Wir spielen gegen blue(euch)." en="We are playing against you (plural)." />
  <Example de="Der Kaffee ist für blue(ihn)." en="The coffee is for him." />
  <Example de="Ich gehe nicht ohne blue(dich)." en="I'm not going without you." />
</ExampleList>

<Callout title="Nicht ohne dich">
  "Ich gehe nicht ohne dich" doesn't negate the whole sentence. I *am* going, just not without you. Only "ohne dich" is negated, so nicht goes right before that part. This is the same as "Ich fahre nicht heute nach Marburg" from the negation lesson.
</Callout>
