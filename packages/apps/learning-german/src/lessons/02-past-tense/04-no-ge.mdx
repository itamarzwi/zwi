## Participles without ge-

Most past participles start with ge-, but there are two groups of verbs that this rule does not apply to:

1. Verbs ending in -ieren.
2. Verbs with inseparable prefixes, such as be-, ver- or er-.

## Verbs ending in -ieren

Most verbs ending in -ieren are regular (weak), and their participle is just the root + -t, without ge-.

| Infinitive   | Participle   | English    |
| ------------ | ------------ | ---------- |
| studieren    | studiert     | to study   |
| probieren    | probiert     | to try     |
| passieren    | passiert     | to happen  |
| trainieren   | trainiert    | to train   |
| reservieren  | reserviert   | to reserve |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Anna hat in Marburg verb(studiert)." en="Anna studied in Marburg." />
  <Example de="Jess hat gestern verb(trainiert)." en="Jess trained yesterday." />
  <Example de="Wir haben einen Tisch verb(reserviert)." en="We reserved a table." />
</ExampleList>

passieren (to happen) comes with "sein". You will see it most often in question form:

<Example de="Was verb(ist) verb(passiert)?" en="What happened?" />

## Inseparable prefixes

Some verbs have what is called an inseparable prefix. Verbs with these prefixes don't get "ge-" in their past participle form either. The most common ones are be-, ver- er-, ent-, and ge-.

If the verb is regular, the participle ends in -t:

| Infinitive   | Participle   | English  |
| ------------ | ------------ | -------- |
| besuchen     | besucht      | to visit |
| bezahlen     | bezahlt      | to pay   |
| entspannen   | entspannt    | to relax |
| verkaufen    | verkauft     | to sell  |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wir haben Anna verb(besucht)." en="We visited Anna." />
  <Example de="Jess hat den Kaffee verb(bezahlt)." en="Jess paid for the coffee." />
  <Example de="Ich habe mein Auto verb(verkauft)." en="I sold my car." />
</ExampleList>

If the verb is irregular, it ends in -en. These verbs mostly build on top of other verbs, so you can take the participle from the base verb and remove the ge-:

| Infinitive  | Comes from            | Participle  | English       |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| verstehen   | stehen → gestanden    | verstanden  | to understand |
| bekommen    | kommen → gekommen     | bekommen    | to get        |
| verschlafen | schlafen → geschlafen | verschlafen | to oversleep  |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe das nicht verb(verstanden)." en="I didn't understand that." />
  <Example de="Anna hat ein Buch verb(bekommen)." en="Anna got a book." />
  <Example de="Jess hat heute verb(verschlafen)." en="Jess overslept today." />
</ExampleList>

Notice that bekommen and verschlafen look exactly the same as the infinitive. The haben in second position tells you it is the Perfekt.

<Callout title={`"bekommen" - A false friend!`}>
"bekommen" does **not** mean "to become", it means "to get" or "to receive". "Ich habe ein Buch bekommen" means "I got a book".
</Callout>
