## The Perfekt tense

When talking about the past in German, the most commonly used tense is called the **Perfekt**, it is the equivalent of the present perfect case in English. It is the tense used in daily conversation, texts, and articles.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) Pizza verb(gekauft)." en="I verb(bought) pizza." />
  <Example de="Wir verb(haben) Deutsch verb(gelernt)." en="We verb(learned) German." />
  <Example de="Anna verb(hat) in Marburg verb(gewohnt)." en="Anna verb(lived) in Marburg." />
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) das schon verb(gemacht)." en="I verb(have) already verb(done) that." />
</ExampleList>

The Perfekt is made of two parts: a form of **haben**, and a special form of the main verb called the **participle**¹. In "Ich habe Pizza gekauft", the participle is "gekauft".

<Callout title="One German tense covers two English ones">
"Ich habe Pizza gekauft" can be translated as either "I bought pizza" or "I have bought pizza". In everyday German, we don't make that distinction, the Perfekt covers both cases. That's why most of the examples above use "habe" in German, but don't use "have" in English.

"I have bought pizza" is a correct translation too, but a rather unusual one. The translations above are for the most natural sounding text, both in English and in German. Sometimes that is the past simple ("I bought pizza"), and sometimes it is the present perfect ("I have already done that").
</Callout>

<Footnote>1. In German, this form is called the Partizip II. It is the equivalent of the English past participle, and it is the form of the verb used in the Perfekt.</Footnote>

## The sentence bracket

In a Perfekt sentence, the main verb is (usually¹) "haben". It gets conjugated as a normal verb, and it comes in second position as always. The participle always comes at the **very end** of the sentence. Everything else goes between the two.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) Pizza verb(gekauft)." en="I bought pizza." />
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) heute Pizza verb(gekauft)." en="I bought pizza today." />
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) heute Pizza zum Abendessen verb(gekauft)." en="I bought pizza for dinner today." />
</ExampleList>

Because the two verbs, haben and the participle, wrap the sentence like brackets, this is called the sentence bracket (**die Satzklammer**). We will see it again later with other types of sentences.

The main verb in this sentence bracket is "haben", and so only it changes forms. The participle never changes!

| German | English |
| ------ | ------- |
| ich habe gemacht | I did |
| du hast gemacht | you did |
| er/sie/es hat gemacht | he/she/it did |
| wir haben gemacht | we did |
| ihr habt gemacht | you (all) did |
| sie/Sie haben gemacht | they/you (formal) did |

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistake">
The participle **always** comes at the end! "Ich habe gekauft Pizza" is wrong. It must be "Ich habe Pizza gekauft"
</Callout>

### The verb stays in second position

The rules about the sentence structure still apply. If the sentence starts with a time or a place, "haben", which acts as the main verb of the sentence, stays in the second position and the subject comes right after it. The participle always stays at the end.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="timeplace(Heute) verb(habe) ich Pizza verb(gekauft)." en="timeplace(Today) I bought pizza." />
  <Example de="timeplace(In Marburg) verb(haben) wir Deutsch verb(gelernt)." en="timeplace(In Marburg) we learned German." />
</ExampleList>

<Footnote>1. Some verbs use "sein" instead, like "Ich bin nach Marburg gefahren". We will learn about those verbs in the lesson "The Perfekt with sein".</Footnote>

## Regular participles

Most verbs are regular, and their participle is built the same way: ge- + the root + -t.

| Infinitive | Root | Participle |
| ---------- | ---- | ---------- |
| machen | mach- | gemacht |
| kaufen | kauf- | gekauft |
| wohnen | wohn- | gewohnt |
| lernen | lern- | gelernt |
| spielen | spiel- | gespielt |
| kochen | koch- | gekocht |
| hören | hör- | gehört |
| sagen | sag- | gesagt |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Wir haben Fußball verb(gespielt)." en="We played football." />
  <Example de="Jess hat Pasta verb(gekocht)." en="Jess cooked pasta." />
  <Example de="Hast du das verb(gehört)?" en="Did you hear that?" />
</ExampleList>

### Roots ending in -t or -d

If the root ends in -t or -d, an extra -e- goes before the last -t. "Gearbeitt" would be impossible to pronounce. It is the same thing that happens in the present tense with "er arbeitet".

| Infinitive | Root | Participle |
| ---------- | ---- | ---------- |
| arbeiten | arbeit- | gearbeitet |
| kosten | kost- | gekostet |
| warten | wart- | gewartet |
| reden | red- | geredet |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe heute nicht verb(gearbeitet)." en="I didn't work today." />
  <Example de="Das Buch hat zehn Euro verb(gekostet)." en="The book cost ten euros." />
</ExampleList>

## Past time words

To say *when* something happened, you need to know some past time words:

| German | English |
| ------ | ------- |
| gestern | yesterday |
| vorgestern | the day before yesterday |
| letzte Woche | last week |
| letztes Jahr | last year |
| am Wochenende | on the weekend |
| vor zwei Tagen | two days ago |


<Callout title="Why Tagen?">
  The plural of "der Tag" is "die Tage", so why "vor zwei Tagen"? Because "vor" here is followed by the **Dativ** case, and in Dativ we add -n to plural nouns. You will learn why in the Dativ module. For now, you can learn it as a fixed phrase: vor zwei Tagen, vor drei Tagen.
</Callout>

They work exactly like "heute" or "morgen": they come right after the verb in the middle of the sentence, or at the start, with the verb right after them.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Anna hat timeplace(letzte Woche) viel gearbeitet." en="Anna worked a lot timeplace(last week)." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Gestern) habe ich Pizza bestellt." en="timeplace(Yesterday) I ordered pizza." />
  <Example de="timeplace(Am Wochenende) haben wir Fußball gespielt." en="timeplace(On the weekend) we played football ." />
  <Example de="Jess hat timeplace(vor zwei Tagen) Pasta gekocht." en="Jess cooked pasta timeplace(two days ago)." />
</ExampleList>
