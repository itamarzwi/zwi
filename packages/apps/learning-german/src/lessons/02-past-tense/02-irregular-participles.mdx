## Irregular participles

Some verbs don't follow the ge- + root + -t pattern. Instead, their participle ends in -en instead of -t. Often the vowel in the middle changes too.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe Pizza verb(gegessen)." en="I ate pizza." note="essen → gegessen" />
  <Example de="Wir haben Kaffee verb(getrunken)." en="We drank coffee." note="trinken → getrunken" />
  <Example de="Anna hat eine E-Mail verb(geschrieben)." en="Anna wrote an email." note="schreiben → geschrieben" />
</ExampleList>

These are called irregular verbs or strong verbs. The only different thing about thim is their participle form. Everything else about the Perfekt stays the same: haben in second position and the participle at the end.

Unfortunately, there is no rule that tells you whether a verb is regular or irregular, so you have to learn the irregular participle together with its verb :]  
The good news is that there are some patterns about what form those irregular verbs take in their Perfekt form!

## Vowel patterns

The vowel changes in the Partizip II form of strong verbs aren't random. Most fall into a few groups, which makes them easier to remember:

| Pattern | Examples |
| ------- | -------- |
| ei → ie | schreiben → geschrieben |
| i → u | trinken → getrunken, finden → gefunden |
| e → o | sprechen → gesprochen, treffen → getroffen, nehmen → genommen |

nehmen is a little *extra irregular*: besides the vowel, the h becomes mm.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Hast du den Film verb(gesehen)?" en="Did you see the film?" />
  <Example de="Ich habe gestern gut verb(geschlafen)." en="I slept well yesterday." />
  <Example de="Jess hat seinen Schlüssel verb(gefunden)." en="Jess found his key." />
  <Example de="Wir haben Deutsch verb(gesprochen)." en="We spoke German." />
  <Example de="Anna hat den Bus verb(genommen)." en="Anna took the bus." />
</ExampleList>

<Callout>
 Some irregular verbs chage their form in the present tense. For example "fahren->du fährst", "schlafen->er schläft". Those verbs end in -en in their Partizip II form:
 | Present tense | Participle |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| er isst | gegessen |
| er liest | gelesen |
| sie schläft | geschlafen |
| sie spricht | gesprochen |
| es nimmt | genommen |

This only works one way, though. Plenty of irregular verbs don't change form in the present tense
</Callout>
