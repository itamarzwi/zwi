## The Perfekt with sein

A small group of verbs come with "sein" in the Perfekt instead of haben. Everything else stays the same: sein goes in second position, the participle goes to the end.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich verb(bin) nach Marburg verb(gefahren)." en="I drove to Marburg." />
  <Example de="Wir verb(sind) nach Hause verb(gegangen)." en="We went home." />
  <Example de="Anna verb(ist) nach Litauen verb(geflogen)." en="Anna flew to Lithuania." />
</ExampleList>

There isn't one specific rule for which verbs come with sein in the Perfekt, but almost all verbs that do belong in one of two groups: movement verbs (Bewegungsverben) and verbs of state change (Vorgangsverben).

### Movement verbs (Bewegungsverben)

Movement verbs are simple, someone or something goes Bewegungsverb. You already know their participles from the list:

| Infinitiv | Partizip II |
| --------- | ----------- |
| gehen     | gegangen    |
| fahren    | gefahren    |
| fliegen   | geflogen    |
| kommen    | gekommen    |

### Verbs of state change (Vorgangsverben)

Verbs of state change are a little trickier, but follow a similar principle. They describe someone or something becoming different: the subject is in one state before, and in a different state after. Like with movement verbs, there is no object. The change just happens to the subject.

| English   | Infinitiv | Partizip II |
| --------- | --------- | ----------- |
| to become | werden    | geworden    |
| to grow   | wachsen   | gewachsen   |
| to die    | sterben   | gestorben   |

All three examples also happen to be irregular! Werden and sterben follow the e → o pattern, and wachsen has a different present form (Du wächst).

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Anna verb(ist) Lehrerin verb(geworden)." en="Anna became a teacher." />
  <Example de="Das Kind verb(ist) groß verb(geworden)." en="The child got big." />
  <Example de="Mein Hund verb(ist) letztes Jahr verb(gestorben)." en="My dog died last year." />
</ExampleList>

## Sein form

"sein" changes its form with the person, and the participle stays the same:

| German                 | English                 |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| ich bin gefahren       | I drove                 |
| du bist gefahren       | you drove               |
| er/sie/es ist gefahren | he/she/it drove         |
| wir sind gefahren      | we drove                |
| ihr seid gefahren      | you (all) drove         |
| sie/Sie sind gefahren  | they/you (formal) drove |

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Er ist gestern nach Hause verb(gegangen)." en="He went home yesterday." />
  <Example de="Seid ihr nach Marburg verb(gefahren)?" en="Did you (all) drive to Marburg?" />
  <Example de="Letztes Jahr sind wir nach Litauen verb(geflogen)." en="Last year we flew to Lithuania." />
</ExampleList>

## bleiben, passieren

There are two more exceptions that you should know about, which are bleiben (to stay) and passieren (to happen). They don't fit the 2 groups, but still come with "sein" in the Perfekt.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich bin zu Hause verb(geblieben)." en="I stayed at home." />
  <Example de="Anna ist zwei Tage in Marburg verb(geblieben)." en="Anna stayed in Marburg for two days." />
  <Example de="Was ist verb(passiert)?" en="What happened?" />
</ExampleList>

<Callout title="passiert - A new exception">
"passiert" is both the present tense form (es passiert) and the Partizip II. So the only difference between "Was passiert?" (What is happening?) and "Was ist passiert?" (What happened?) is the "ist".

You will see this again in the next lesson: regular verbs without ge- that have a Partizip II that looks exactly like their er/sie/es form, like "er studiert" and "er hat studiert".
</Callout>

## haben or sein?

A verb only comes with sein in the Perfekt, if it matches any of the rules we just learned: does the subject move Bewegungsverb, or change from one state to another? If yes, it is sein. bleiben and passieren are exceptions that also use sein. Everything else¹ uses haben.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich verb(habe) in Marburg verb(gewohnt)." en="I lived in Marburg." />
  <Example de="Ich verb(bin) nach Marburg verb(gefahren)." en="I drove to Marburg." note="Bewegungsverb (Movement)" />
  <Example de="Wir verb(haben) Pizza verb(gegessen)." en="We ate pizza." />
  <Example de="Wir verb(sind) nach Hause verb(gegangen)." en="We went home." note="Bewegungsverb (Movement)" />
  <Example de="Das Kind verb(hat) gut verb(geschlafen)." en="The child slept well." />
  <Example de="Das Kind verb(ist) groß verb(geworden)." en="The child got big." note="Vorgangsverb (Change of state)" />
</ExampleList>

<Footnote>1. There are a few other exceptions that use sein without belonging to either group, but bleiben and passieren are the only ones that you need for now.</Footnote>
