## Yes/no questions

Yes/no questions work just like in the present tense: the conjugated verb moves to the first position. In the Perfekt, that is haben or sein. The participle stays at the end.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Du verb(hast) Pizza verb(gekauft)." en="You bought pizza." />
  <Example de="verb(Hast) du Pizza verb(gekauft)?" en="Did you buy pizza?" />
</ExampleList>

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Anna verb(ist) nach Marburg verb(gefahren)." en="Anna drove to Marburg." />
  <Example de="verb(Ist) Anna nach Marburg verb(gefahren)?" en="Did Anna drive to Marburg?" />
</ExampleList>

With war and hatte, there is only one verb, so it simply moves to the front:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="verb(Warst) du gestern in Marburg?" en="Were you in Marburg yesterday?" />
  <Example de="verb(Hattest) du Zeit?" en="Did you have time?" />
</ExampleList>

## W-questions

W-questions also work like in the present tense: the question word comes first, then the conjugated verb. The participle still goes to the end.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Was verb(hast) du gestern verb(gemacht)?" en="What did you do yesterday?" />
  <Example de="Wann verb(bist) du nach Hause verb(gekommen)?" en="When did you come home?" />
  <Example de="Wer verb(hat) das verb(gesagt)?" en="Who said that?" />
  <Example de="Wo verb(warst) du?" en="Where were you?" />
  <Example de="Wie verb(war) der Film?" en="How was the film?" />
</ExampleList>

## Negation

### nicht

In the Perfekt, nicht goes right before the participle, at the end of the sentence.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe das Buch adverb(nicht) gelesen." en="I didn't read the book." />
  <Example de="Anna hat gestern adverb(nicht) gearbeitet." en="Anna didn't work yesterday." />
  <Example de="Wir haben das adverb(nicht) verstanden." en="We didn't understand that." />
</ExampleList>

The other rules from the negation lesson still apply. For example, nicht still goes before a place:

<Example de="Ich bin adverb(nicht) nach Marburg gefahren." en="I didn't drive to Marburg." />

With war, nicht goes where it would with ist:

<Example de="Der Film war adverb(nicht) gut." en="The film was not good." />

### kein

kein works the same as always: it replaces ein, or goes in front of a noun with no article.

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Ich habe adverb(keinen) Kaffee getrunken." en="I didn't drink coffee." />
  <Example de="Wir hatten adverb(keine) Zeit." en="We had no time." />
  <Example de="Jess hat adverb(kein) Buch gekauft." en="Jess didn't buy a book." />
</ExampleList>

### Doch

doch also works with past questions:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Hast du nicht gegessen?" en="Didn't you eat?" />
  <Example de="adverb(Doch), ich habe gegessen." en="Yes, I did eat." />
</ExampleList>

## Was hast du am Wochenende gemacht?

This is one of the most common questions in German, especially on a Monday morning. Here is how a conversation about the weekend might go:

<ExampleList>
  <Example de="Jess: Hallo Anna! Wie war dein Wochenende?" en="Jess: Hi Anna! How was your weekend?" />
  <Example de="Anna: Gut, danke! Am Samstag war ich in Marburg." en="Anna: Good, thanks! On Saturday I was in Marburg." />
  <Example de="Jess: Was hast du da gemacht?" en="Jess: What did you do there?" />
  <Example de="Anna: Ich habe eine Freundin besucht. Wir haben Kaffee getrunken und viel geredet." en="Anna: I visited a friend. We drank coffee and talked a lot." />
  <Example de="Jess: Und am Sonntag?" en="Jess: And on Sunday?" />
  <Example de="Anna: Am Sonntag bin ich zu Hause geblieben und habe ein Buch gelesen. Und du?" en="Anna: On Sunday I stayed at home and read a book. And you?" />
  <Example de="Jess: Ich hatte keine Zeit. Ich habe am Samstag und am Sonntag gearbeitet." en="Jess: I had no time. I worked on Saturday and on Sunday." />
  <Example de="Anna: Oh nein! Hast du nicht geschlafen?" en="Anna: Oh no! Didn't you sleep?" />
  <Example de="Jess: Doch, aber nicht viel." en="Jess: Yes, I did, but not much." />
</ExampleList>

Notice how they use war and hatte for sein and haben, and the Perfekt for everything else, with haben for most verbs and sein for bleiben.
