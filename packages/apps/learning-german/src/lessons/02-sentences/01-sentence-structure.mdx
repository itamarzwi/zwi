## Subject, verb, object

The simplest German sentence is **subject - verb - object**, just like English. Some sentences need only a subject and a verb.

<Example de="Ich lerne Deutsch." en="I am learning German." note="subject - verb - object" />
<Example de="Anna schläft." en="Anna is sleeping." note="subject - verb" />

## Adjectives

After a form of **sein**, an adjective stays unchanged:

<Example de="Der Kaffee ist heiß." en="The coffee is hot." />
<Example de="Die Stadt ist groß." en="The city is big." />

## Time and place

You can add *when* and *where*. In the middle of a sentence, **time comes before place**.

<Example de="Ich arbeite heute in Berlin." en="I am working in Berlin today." note="time (heute) before place (in Berlin)" />

## The verb stays in second position

You can put time or place at the **start** of the sentence. The verb still has to be the **second element**, so the subject moves behind it.

<Example de="Heute arbeite ich in Berlin." en="Today I am working in Berlin." />
<Example de="In Berlin arbeite ich heute." en="In Berlin I am working today." />

<Callout type="warning" title="Common mistake">
"Heute ich arbeite" is wrong. Verb second, always: **Heute arbeite ich**.
</Callout>
